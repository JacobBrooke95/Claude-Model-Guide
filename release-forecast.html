Claude Release Forecast

When will Anthropic ship its next model in each family? These are probabilities from a backtested statistical model, not a release schedule.

The forecast

Each card gives the probability that the family's next model is publicly released by the date shown. The big number is the model's own estimate, using cadence, calendar and pre-release signals. The market column is the Polymarket price where a market exists. The blend averages the two in log-odds with equal weight.

Cumulative probability, next 120 days

Lines show the model's P(released by date). Circles show Polymarket "released by" prices after a monotone fit, because thin daily rungs are sometimes out of order. Shading marks US election days (even years), Thanksgiving Thursday–Friday and the Dec 20 – Jan 4 freeze, where the model cuts the daily hazard to a quarter.

Signals the model is reading

What the next release will look like

Release parameters beyond the date. Each line says whether the estimate is fitted from data (and from how many points) or rule-based.

Names: the step sizes the family has used since Claude 4, plus the generation clock; for Fable and Mythos, a rule gives 85% to the version named in the Azure row and the commit trailer. Price: the share of launches since 2025 that kept, cut or raised list price (n in brackets). Co-launch: the share of the family's releases that shared a day with another family. SWE-bench Pro: Anthropic-reported scores, projected by pooling the per-release rate of progress across families. Treat it as a rough extrapolation on a benchmark nearing saturation.

When does generation 6 arrive?

The generation clock fits a log-normal to the four gaps between generation debuts (Claude 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5, where 5 means Fable 5 on Jun 9, 2026), conditioned on no Claude 6 yet. With four data points the band is wide, but it lands close to both markets.

Calibration by period (final model, 30-day questions)

Head-to-head tests

Opus, Sonnet and Haiku questions. Each row gives the mean Brier difference per block (A − B; negative means A is better). Significance treats each inter-release interval as one observation, so n is the number of blocks, close to the number of release events. The 95% interval bootstraps those blocks, and p is a t-test on the block means. Green means the interval favours A; red means it favours B.

Inputs, and what each one is worth

Every input the forecaster knows about, sorted into seven categories. Status says whether the input is fitted from data, set as a prior or rule, tested and found empty, or watched only.

Signal classes, as estimated today

Detection rate is the share of the past year's releases that had a signal of this class. Typical lead is the median days from first sighting to release, from the log-normal fit. False alarms are counted per family-day. Classes share sources, so the model shrinks their combined weight (ρ = 0.5) and caps each class's likelihood ratio at 5×.
